New CSIC report

The essentials in 2 minutes

The Pyrenean Institute of Ecology (IPE-CSIC) has measured what falls from the air at four sites in Bilbao. This is the summary; the full 15-page report, in Spanish, can be downloaded here.

Download the report · PDF

What was measured

  • Atmospheric deposition: what falls from the air and settles on the ground, on window sills and on washing hung out to dry.
  • Four sites: Zorroza, Ribera de Deusto, San Ignacio and the centre of Bilbao as a comparison.
  • 56 days, from 30 April to 25 June 2026, with collectors looked after by residents.
  • Up to 69 components analysed in the IPE-CSIC laboratories. Author of the report: Jorge Pey Betrán.

Over San Ignacio, 8.9 micrograms of lead were deposited per square metre per day; the maximum for the reference cities is 2.2.

In Ribera de Deusto and San Ignacio, 17 trace elements exceed that maximum; in Zorroza, 7; in the centre of Bilbao, 4, generally related to traffic.

Where it may come from

The report links these elements to three types of source: industrial processes, with both channelled and diffuse emissions; fuel-oil combustion; and road traffic.

Molybdenum is high at all four sites, including the centre: the report considers it a probable metallurgical signal of regional origin.

What the report does not say

  • It is an exploratory study: 56 days and four sites.
  • It does not attribute the exceedances to a specific source.
  • The reference cities are Barcelona, Zaragoza, Palma and Pamplona, with data from 2016 to 2025.
  • Spain has no legal limit for metal deposition. Germany allows 100 µg/m²·day of lead as an annual mean; 8.9 was measured here.

What we are asking for now

  • Chromium speciation: to know whether what falls on our homes is the carcinogenic kind.
  • An epidemiological study of the affected neighbourhoods.
  • Fixed monitors that work and that count in the official assessment, and public emissions data.
