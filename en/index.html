Where it is

A hazardous waste plant in the middle of the city

Sader has treated hazardous industrial waste at Punta Zorroza since 1986. Next door, Profersa makes fertilisers from waste. Both belong to the Agaleus group. San Ignacio lies directly opposite, across the estuary; Zorroza, behind; Ribera de Deusto, downwind.

Summary

Nine findings

The essentials of the report. Each finding takes you to the chapter with all the data, the nuances and the sources.

One of the largest hazardous waste plants in Spain operates inside Bilbao

79,593 tonnes of hazardous waste in 2025, about 500 metres from three schools. The company itself claims to have the largest waste treatment capacity in the whole of northern Spain.

Chapter 2 →

The inspection examined the quantities treated in 2022 and 2023

Sader received 86,328 and 80,117 tonnes of hazardous waste, against a limit of 79,140. Although the specific limit for hazardous waste was exceeded, the Inspectorate concluded that there was no breach when the total authorised capacity was taken into account.

Chapter 3 →

Afterwards, its capacity was increased by 38%

On 17 March 2025, as a “non-substantial modification”: with no public consultation, no dispersion study and no report from the Health Department, according to the residents' complaint.

Chapter 3 →

It is permitted to take 2,000 tonnes a year of hexavalent chromium

A carcinogen. That capacity did not increase with the 2025 expansion. In 2024 it treated 388 tonnes and in 2025, 110. The risk lies in transporting, unloading and handling those baths next to homes.

Chapter 3 →

More metal falls on San Ignacio and Deusto than in any city measured in Spain

4 times more lead, 3.7 times more zinc and twice the chromium of the worst urban setting. In the centre of Bilbao, measured at the same time, this does not happen. (The study compares the samples with selected urban references; the results need context and do not, on their own, allow a specific source to be identified.)

Chapter 4 →

The soil next to the plant exceeds the legal value for a park

9 times over for arsenic, 6 for lead and 4 for nickel. In January 2026 the administration required Sader to submit the soil status report, which according to the environmental authority had not been submitted.

Chapter 5 →

Ten years with no Basque Government station in Zorroza and San Ignacio

From 2014 to 2024. The mobile unit in San Ignacio, installed in 2024, records 70% more fine particles than the WHO considers healthy. Since September 2026 it has not been working properly.

Chapter 6 →

A specific epidemiological study for the neighbourhoods is being requested

No specific, public epidemiological study has been found in the sources consulted. Access to the emission measurements and their methodology is also being requested.

Chapter 9 →

The move to the port is a permit, not an order

The Zierbena permit allows until 10 June 2031 to start operating there. It does not impose the closure of Zorroza. Profersa is not included.

Conclusion

Chapter 1 · The plant

What Sader is and where it is

Sader (Sociedad Anónima de Descontaminación y Eliminación de Residuos) is a treatment plant for hazardous and non-hazardous industrial waste at Punta Zorroza, on the left bank of the Bilbao estuary. The company was incorporated in 1986; its current integrated environmental permit was granted on 14 January 2008 and reviewed in 2022.[26,9] It belongs to the Agaleus group, as does Profersa, the adjoining factory that makes fertilisers from waste and was spun off from Sader in 2012 with a permit of its own.[9,22] The press has described it as the last major polluting industry left inside the built-up area of Bilbao.[22,27]

Geography explains the conflict. San Ignacio lies directly opposite, across the estuary; Zorroza, behind; Ribera de Deusto, where new homes are being handed over, downwind of the prevailing winds. Basurto, Altamira, Ibarrekolanda and Burtzeña (Barakaldo) are affected as well.[22,28] In the Basque Parliament, the number of people exposed to persistent odours and poor air quality has been put at around 11,000.[8]

In the meantime

What you can do to protect yourself

Lead, arsenic, nickel and chromium have been measured next to the plant, in the soil and in what falls from the air. Ambient dust can be a route of contact with some substances. The environmental measurements cited do not, on their own, make it possible to quantify individual exposure or to identify a single source. These general habits can help to limit contact with dust.

Wash your hands when you get in

With soap and water, as soon as you come home and before eating or cooking. And always wash children's hands when they come back from the park or the playground.

Why: Dust containing metals sticks to the skin and ends up in the mouth. It is the most important route of exposure to lead in childhood.

Take your shoes off at the door

Leave outdoor shoes in the hallway and wear slippers indoors. Put one doormat outside and another inside.

Why: Soles carry dust from the street and from contaminated soil onto the carpets and floors where children play.

Do not hang washing outside

Especially on days when the wind blows towards your neighbourhood, or when there is a smell or visible dust. Dry it indoors or use a tumble dryer. If you do hang it outside, shake it before putting it away.

Why: Damp clothes trap airborne particles and you then wear them, against your skin for hours.

Clean with a damp cloth, do not sweep

Mop and damp cloth for floors, furniture, window sills and balconies. Avoid brooms and feather dusters. If you have a vacuum cleaner with a HEPA filter, use it.

Why: Dry sweeping puts the dust back into the air you breathe. With a damp cloth it stays on the cloth.

Air your home sensibly

Air rooms briefly and thoroughly (five or ten minutes). Avoid opening windows on days with a smell, dust or wind from the plant, and close them if there is an episode.

Why: Renewing the air at home is necessary, but not when what comes in is worse than what goes out.

Look after the little ones

Wash their hands often, keep their nails short, wipe their outdoor toys with a damp cloth and do not let them eat in the park without washing first.

Why: Children put their hands in their mouths, breathe more air per kilo of body weight, and their nervous system is more sensitive to lead.

Vegetable plots and pots: take care

Wash very thoroughly (and peel if you can) anything you grow on the balcony or in nearby allotments. Do not grow food directly in local soil without having it analysed.

Why: Lead and arsenic have been measured in the soil next to the plant at levels far above the legal value for a park.

Clean paws

If you have a dog, wipe its paws with a damp cloth when you come back from a walk.

Why: Your pets bring dust from the ground into the house too.

An air purifier, if you can

If you can afford one, a purifier with a HEPA filter in the bedroom reduces fine particles indoors. Change the filter when it is due.

Why: It helps inside the home, but it is no substitute for the rest, or for removing the source.
If there is an episode

A cloud, a strong smell or visible dust

No specific external emergency plan or public warning protocol has been found in the available sources; it needs to be clarified which are legally required and which are in force. In May 2024, people who called 112 were told to close doors and windows.

  1. Close doors and windows and switch off any appliance that draws in outside air (extractor fans, air conditioning).
  2. Call 112 if you see smoke or a cloud, or if someone feels unwell.
  3. Document it: a photo or video with the time, the place you are filming from and the direction the wind is blowing. One dated piece of evidence is worth more than ten complaints.
  4. Report it to the Basque Government's Environmental Inspectorate and share it in the WhatsApp community: we add it to the residents' log.
  5. If you have symptoms (irritated eyes or throat, a cough, difficulty breathing), go to your health centre and mention the exposure. That way it is on record.

These are general hygiene tips for reducing exposure to dust, not medical advice. If you have doubts about your health or your children's, ask your doctor or paediatrician. And remember: protecting yourself at home helps, but the solution is not in your mop. It is in not having a hazardous waste plant next to people's homes.
